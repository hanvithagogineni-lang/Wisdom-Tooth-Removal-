Identify The Signs

Is Your Wisdom Tooth Causing Trouble?

Wisdom teeth often erupt between ages 17 and 25. Due to limited jaw space, they can cause discomfort, alignment issues, or pain. Recognizing symptoms early prevents secondary dental complications.

Tooth Pain

Persistent, pulsating, or recurring pain around the back of your jaw near the molar region.

Swollen Gums

Tender, reddened, or inflamed gum flaps around a partially erupted wisdom tooth.

Jaw Discomfort

Stiffness, tightness, or pain when opening your mouth, chewing, or yawning.

Impacted Tooth

A tooth trapped beneath the gum line or pressing sideways against neighboring healthy molars.

Food Trapping

Partially emerged teeth create deep pockets where food particles collect, leading to bad breath.

Repeated Infections

Recurring localized swelling or pericoronitis infection that requires specialized clinical management.

Do I Really Need Wisdom Tooth Removal?

Not every wisdom tooth requires extraction! At Dantam Clinics, we believe in conservative, evidence-based dentistry. Treatment recommendations depend on comprehensive examination and imaging.

Aftercare Expectations

What to Expect After Wisdom Tooth Removal

Healing timelines vary by individual and procedure complexity. Here is general aftercare guidance.

Day 1

First 24 Hours

Keep initial gauze in place as directed. Rest, avoid strenuous activity, and refrain from spitting or using straws to protect the clot.

Days 2 - 3

Next Few Days

Mild swelling or jaw tenderness is normal. Apply cold compresses, eat soft foods (curd rice, smoothies, soups), and take prescribed medication.

Days 4 - 7

During Recovery

Swelling subsides. Gently rinse with warm salt water after meals to maintain clean oral hygiene around the healing area.

Understanding Potential Risks & Safety

Wisdom tooth removal is a routine oral surgical procedure. Being informed about potential temporary effects builds trust through transparency.

Swelling & Tenderness

Normal tissue response that peaks within 48 hours and subsides with cold packs and medication.

Minor Bleeding

Slight oozing for the first 12-24 hours is standard and managed with gentle pressure gauze.

Dry Socket

Occurs if the blood clot dislodges early. Prevented by following aftercare instructions carefully.

Temporary Stiffness

Temporary limitation in mouth opening (trismus) that resolves as jaw muscles recover.

Your dental professional will review your individual scan and discuss any specific precautions before proceeding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about wisdom tooth removal in Hyderabad.

No, wisdom tooth removal is not always required. If your wisdom tooth is fully erupted, correctly positioned, healthy, and easy to keep clean, it can be left alone and monitored during routine checkups. Removal is recommended only when teeth are impacted, causing pain, infection, or threatening neighboring molars.

Symptoms include jaw pain or stiffness, swollen or bleeding gums around the back molars, difficulty opening your mouth, unpleasant taste or bad breath from trapped food, and headaches radiating from the jaw area.

During the procedure, local anesthesia completely numbs the treatment area, so you will not feel pain—only mild pressure. After the procedure, any tenderness or swelling is managed effectively with prescribed pain relief medications.

A simple extraction usually takes 15 to 30 minutes. A surgical extraction for an impacted tooth may take 30 to 60 minutes depending on tooth complexity and root orientation.

Simple extractions are performed on wisdom teeth that are fully visible above the gum line. Surgical extractions are required for wisdom teeth trapped under the gum or bone, or growing at difficult angles.

An OPG (panoramic X-ray) is recommended to view all wisdom teeth and jaw structures. If the roots are close to the inferior alveolar nerve, a 3D CBCT scan may be advised for safer surgical planning.

Stick to soft, cool foods for the first few days, such as yogurt, smooth soups, ice cream, mashed potatoes, and curd rice. Avoid hot, spicy, crunchy, or chewy foods, and do not use a straw.

Most patients resume normal work or college activities within 1 to 3 days. Complete gum tissue healing typically takes 1 to 2 weeks.
